Clear WRITING CASES Sentence case

Capitalizes the first letter of each sentence. Example: "hello world." becomes "Hello world."

lower case

Makes every single letter lowercase. Example: "HELLO WORLD" becomes "hello world".

UPPER CASE

Makes every single letter uppercase. Example: "hello world" becomes "HELLO WORLD".

Title Case

Capitalizes the first letter of each major word. Articles (a, an, the) and short prepositions stay lowercase unless they are the first word. Example: "the quick brown fox" becomes "The Quick Brown Fox".

aLtErNaTiNg cAsE

Alternates between lowercase and uppercase for each character. Example: "hello" becomes "hElLo". Often used for mocking or comedic effect online.

InVeRsE CaSe

Flips the case of every letter - uppercase becomes lowercase, and lowercase becomes uppercase. Example: "Hello World" becomes "hELLO wORLD".

PROGRAMMING CASES camelCase

Removes all spaces and capitalizes the first letter of every word after the first. Used in JavaScript, Java, and many languages for variable names. Example: "user first name" becomes "userFirstName".

PascalCase

Like camelCase, but the very first word is also capitalized. Used for class and component names in most languages. Example: "user account" becomes "UserAccount".

snake_case

Replaces all spaces with underscores and lowercases everything. Common in Python, Ruby, and SQL for variable and function names. Example: "user first name" becomes "user_first_name".

SCREAMING_SNAKE

snake_case but fully uppercase. Used for constants in most programming languages. Example: "max retry count" becomes "MAX_RETRY_COUNT".

kebab-case

Replaces all spaces with hyphens and lowercases everything. The standard for URLs, CSS class names, and HTML attributes. Example: "user first name" becomes "user-first-name".

What is the difference between Title Case and Sentence case? ▼

These two cases are the most commonly confused, especially in professional writing. Sentence case mirrors how you would write a normal sentence: only the very first word is capitalized, along with proper nouns like names and places. For example: "The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog." This format is standard for body text, email subject lines, blog post body paragraphs, and most everyday writing because it is the most natural and readable format for long passages.

Title Case, on the other hand, capitalizes the first letter of every major word in a phrase. Minor words like articles (a, an, the), short prepositions (in, on, at), and coordinating conjunctions (and, but, or) are generally kept lowercase unless they appear at the very beginning. For example: "The Quick Brown Fox Jumps Over the Lazy Dog." Title Case is the standard for book titles, movie names, article headings, app names, and navigation menus. When in doubt, use Sentence case for writing and Title Case for headings.
